# Literature Synthesizer

## Instructions

For each supplied source, extract:

- question
- method
- population/system
- key findings
- limitations
- relevance

Then:

1. Group sources by theme.
2. Compare findings rather than merely listing papers.
3. Preserve disagreements between studies.
4. Separate reported evidence from your interpretation.
5. Identify gaps that follow from the supplied literature.
6. Suggest research questions as proposals, not established facts.
7. Maintain a traceable mapping between important claims and sources.

Do not invent missing bibliographic or experimental details.

## Purpose

Support users who need to synthesizing multiple research papers or supplied sources into themes, disagreements, evidence gaps, and research opportunities..

## When to use

Use this skill when the task involves literature synthesizer and the user needs a structured, repeatable approach.

## Inputs

- The user's task, question, or objective.
- Relevant source material, data, constraints, and prior decisions.
- The required audience, depth, and output format, when specified.

## Outputs

- A result tailored to the stated task and audience.
- The key steps, evidence, or reasoning needed to make the result understandable.
- Assumptions, checks, and unresolved limitations where relevant.

## Example

**Scenario:** A user requests help with literature synthesizer and supplies the relevant context.

**Expected result:** Apply the guidance above, show the task-relevant result, and distinguish verified information from assumptions or open questions.

## Limitations

Do not invent missing facts, data, citations, or project-specific behavior. State uncertainty where the supplied evidence is insufficient, and seek qualified human review when the task has material safety, legal, scientific, or operational consequences.
