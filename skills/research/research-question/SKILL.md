# Research Question

Convert vague research goals into an evidence-driven investigation.

## Process

1. Restate the question in operational terms.
2. Define scope, population, time period, and terminology.
3. Break the question into answerable subquestions.
4. Identify which claims require primary sources, reviews, datasets, experiments, or official documentation.
5. Create a search plan using multiple independent source types.
6. Track claims, evidence, contradictions, and unresolved gaps.
7. Distinguish established findings from interpretation and speculation.
8. Produce a synthesis that preserves uncertainty.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer, when applicable:

1. Primary research or original technical documentation
2. Official datasets, standards, or institutional sources
3. Systematic reviews and high-quality surveys
4. Reputable secondary analysis
5. Community discussions for practical experience or discovery

Do not treat popularity as evidence of correctness.

## Output

Return:

- Research question
- Scope and definitions
- Subquestions
- Evidence plan
- Search terms
- Claim/evidence table
- Contradictions and gaps
- Final synthesis

## When to use

Use this skill when the task involves research question and the user needs a structured, repeatable approach.

## Instructions

Apply the guidance and checks above in sequence. Use the supplied context as the source of truth, make assumptions explicit, and verify important conclusions before presenting them.

## Inputs

- The user's task, question, or objective.
- Relevant source material, data, constraints, and prior decisions.
- The required audience, depth, and output format, when specified.

## Outputs

- A result tailored to the stated task and audience.
- The key steps, evidence, or reasoning needed to make the result understandable.
- Assumptions, checks, and unresolved limitations where relevant.

## Example

**Scenario:** A user requests help with research question and supplies the relevant context.

**Expected result:** Apply the guidance above, show the task-relevant result, and distinguish verified information from assumptions or open questions.

## Limitations

Do not invent missing facts, data, citations, or project-specific behavior. State uncertainty where the supplied evidence is insufficient, and seek qualified human review when the task has material safety, legal, scientific, or operational consequences.
