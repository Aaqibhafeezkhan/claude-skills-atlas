# Source Verification

Verify the support behind a claim rather than merely collecting links.

## Process

1. Write the exact claim being checked.
2. Identify the source type and publication date.
3. Locate the original or highest-authority source available.
4. Check whether the source actually supports the claim.
5. Check scope, population, version, and date.
6. Look for credible contradictory evidence when consequential.
7. Record limitations without pretending to have certainty.

## Red flags

- Search-result snippets used as evidence
- Secondary summaries that overstate the original source
- Outdated documentation for changing software
- Claims detached from their original context
- Unclear authorship or provenance
- Statistics without a defined population or time period

## Output

For each claim:

- Claim
- Best source
- Source type and date
- Supporting evidence
- Scope and caveats
- Contradictory evidence
- Verification status

## Purpose

Verify important factual claims before they are repeated in research, technical writing, or documentation. Use when sources may be outdated, secondary, conflicting, or difficult to interpret..

## When to use

Use this skill when the task involves source verification and the user needs a structured, repeatable approach.

## Instructions

Apply the guidance and checks above in sequence. Use the supplied context as the source of truth, make assumptions explicit, and verify important conclusions before presenting them.

## Inputs

- The user's task, question, or objective.
- Relevant source material, data, constraints, and prior decisions.
- The required audience, depth, and output format, when specified.

## Outputs

- A result tailored to the stated task and audience.
- The key steps, evidence, or reasoning needed to make the result understandable.
- Assumptions, checks, and unresolved limitations where relevant.

## Example

**Scenario:** A user requests help with source verification and supplies the relevant context.

**Expected result:** Apply the guidance above, show the task-relevant result, and distinguish verified information from assumptions or open questions.

## Limitations

Do not invent missing facts, data, citations, or project-specific behavior. State uncertainty where the supplied evidence is insufficient, and seek qualified human review when the task has material safety, legal, scientific, or operational consequences.
